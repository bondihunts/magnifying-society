The case

In 1851, Francis O'Brien bought the land around Bondi Beach. For thirty years the O'Brien Estate was his: he opened it to Sunday picnickers, then threatened to shut them out.

In 1882, the government stepped in and made Bondi a public beach. But the O'Brien family kept a claim on the sand, until a single contract signed in 1917 handed the beach to Waverley Council. It sat in the archives for ninety years.

In 2007, a Council clerk named Adam Benett found it, and something in it worried him. He hid the contract and never told anyone where.

Adam died last year. The trail he left behind has resurfaced. The Council wants the contract back, and they've asked for your help.

The experience

Grab your team and head out. The O'Brien File is played outdoors, across Bondi, at your own pace.

Every puzzle is solved by looking around you: a date on a plaque, a detail on a statue, a word painted on a wall you've walked past a hundred times. No trivia, no app to download. Just sharp eyes and good teamwork.

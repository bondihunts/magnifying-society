Things to do in Bondi

Here's how to make the most of a day there, from the classics to the corners locals keep to themselves.

1. Swim, surf or just watch the beach

The kilometre of sand between North Bondi and the Icebergs is the obvious place to start. Bondi is patrolled by professional lifeguards all year: always swim between the red and yellow flags. Want to try surfing? You can book a lesson with Let's Go Surfing, right on the beach. A stroll along the promenade is just as rewarding: the sea wall is covered in colourful street art, and the Bondi Pavilion Gallery hosts regular exhibitions right on the beachfront.

2. Bondi Icebergs and the ocean pool

At the southern end of the beach, the Icebergs pool is the most photographed swimming pool in Australia, with waves breaking right over its edge. Entry is $10, which also gives you access to the café and the sauna. For an extra fee, there's also a gym with an incredible view over the bay. The club next door has one of the best views in Sydney for a drink.

3. Walk the Bondi to Coogee coastal walk

From the Icebergs, the coastal walk follows the cliffs south through Tamarama, Bronte and Clovelly to Coogee: about 6 km and two to three hours with stops.

4. Explore North Bondi and Ben Buckler

Walk to the northern end of the beach and keep going around the headland to Ben Buckler. It's quieter, with rock pools, big ocean views and a more local feel. Near the golf course you can also find Aboriginal rock engravings, a reminder that this coastline has a history going back thousands of years.

5. Solve The O'Brien File

The original Bondi Beach contract is missing. In this 90-minute outdoor mystery game, your group follows the clues through the streets of Bondi, cracks puzzles hidden in real places and uncovers the true story of the families who once owned the beach. It's the best way to see the Bondi most visitors walk straight past, and you don't need to know anything about the area: everything is in the case file. Book your session.

6. Explore Bondi's Art Deco architecture

Bondi boomed between the wars, and many of its apartment buildings still carry the Art Deco style of the period: streamlined forms, geometric patterns and bold colours, with a coastal twist. Wander the backstreets of North Bondi and the town centre, including Hall Street, then finish at the Bondi Pavilion, the beachfront's cultural icon. For a guided route, follow the Bondi Art Deco architecture walk: about an hour from North Bondi to the Pavilion. Most of the buildings are private homes, so admire them from the street.

7. Go shopping on Gould Street and Hall Street

Bondi is also a great place to shop. Gould Street and Hall Street are lined with fashion and lifestyle boutiques, from local labels to beachwear and homewares. On Sundays, don't miss the Bondi Markets, held every week in the grounds of Bondi Beach Public School on Campbell Parade: young designers, vintage finds, jewellery and art, right across the road from the sand.

8. Join Bondi's café culture

Coffee is serious business in Bondi, and the suburb is full of charming cafés. Watch the sunrise over the beach from Birichina, a tiny café perched above the south end. Head to The Depot or Harrys Bondi for brunch in North Bondi, grab a coffee overlooking the Icebergs pool at The Crabbe Hole, or settle in at Café Bikini on Hall Street.

How to get to Bondi from Sydney city centre

There's no train station at the beach itself. The simplest option is the 333 bus, which runs from Circular Quay through the city and Oxford Street to Campbell Parade, right by the sand. You can also take the T4 train to Bondi Junction and change to the 333 or 380 bus for the last 10 to 15 minutes.
